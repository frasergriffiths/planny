Research plan / Shared plots / Harlow Green

Find out what keeps a shared plot working.

Half the plots on two sites change hands every year. Before the council rewrites the tenancy, this study asks plot holders what makes a shared site easy to keep, and what makes people leave.

In short
  • Thirty interviews across eight sites, plus a walk of each site and a read of the last three years of tenancy records.
  • The question is turnover, not gardening. We want the three things that most often push a plot holder to give up.
  • The council needs findings by 12 December, before the tenancy consultation opens.

Why turnover

The waiting list gets the attention. The bigger loss is the people who leave a plot within two years. Harlow Green has 210 plots and a waiting list of 60. It also re-lets 48 plots a year. Most of those are given up, not taken away, and the same sites lose people faster than others. The council is about to rewrite the tenancy agreement and has no evidence on why people leave.

Research question

What makes a shared plot easy to keep for the first two years, and which site conditions most often lead a plot holder to give up?

Outcome

A short report with three findings the council can act on and a clear account of what the study cannot say. No recommendations beyond the evidence.

Why now

The tenancy consultation opens in January. A study finished in December can shape the draft. A study finished in spring arrives after the decision and becomes decoration.

02 Method

Eight sites, thirty interviews

Mixed methods, small sample, honest limits. The study describes eight sites and does not claim to speak for the county.

In scope

  • Eight council sites in Harlow Green, chosen for different turnover rates.
  • Thirty semi-structured interviews, half with people who left in the last two years.
  • A site walk at each location, with water, access, storage and shade recorded.
  • Three years of tenancy records, anonymised before analysis.

Out of scope

  • Private allotment sites and sites outside the district.
  • A survey of the waiting list. That is a separate piece of work.
  • Recommendations on rent or plot size. The evidence will not support either.

04 Report

Write the report and share the evidence

Three findings, each tied to evidence, plus the limits the sample cannot cross.

Deliverables

  • A 12-page report with three findings and the evidence behind each.
  • An anonymised site condition table.
  • A two-page summary for councillors.
  • The interview guide and codebook, published with the report.

Review cadence

  • Tuesday, 15 minutes: Nell and Ruth on recruitment, access and anything blocking fieldwork.
  • Week six: a one-page methods note to Ruth before analysis starts.
  • Week eight: draft to two committees and Ruth, with five working days for corrections.
Done means
  • Thirty interviews are coded and stored anonymised.
  • Every finding cites the evidence behind it.
  • The report names what the sample cannot support.
  • Committees have seen the draft before the council does.
Open questions
  • Do we pay vouchers to former plot holders too?
  • Can the council share records without names?
  • Who owns the interview data after the study ends?
